The city council voted on Tuesday to convert three downtown parking lots into
public green spaces over the next two years. Supporters argued that the parks
would reduce summer heat, give residents more places to gather, and attract
small businesses to the area. Opponents worried about the loss of roughly 400
parking spaces and the impact on shops that depend on drivers.

To address those concerns, the plan includes a new bus route that loops through
downtown every ten minutes and a pilot program offering discounted monthly passes
to people who work in the district. The council also approved funding for 200
bicycle parking spots and a study of a possible parking garage near the train
station.

Construction on the first park is expected to begin next spring. The council said
it will collect feedback from residents through public workshops before finalizing
the designs, and will review traffic and business data after the first year to
decide whether to adjust the remaining phases.
